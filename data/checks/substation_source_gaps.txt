Substation Source Gap Analysis
Generated: 2026-06-09
Script: scripts/compare_substations.py
========================================

Summary: there ARE a few systematic patterns worth knowing. Most missing
substations fall into identifiable categories rather than being random noise.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
PGE: 42 attrs-only (not in loads)
─────────────────────────────────────────────
38 of 42 (90%) are flagged redacted="Yes" in pge_substation_attributes.csv.
PGE publishes capacity attributes for redacted substations in GRIP but omits
their load profiles from the map layer. The remaining 4 are likely name
mismatches (e.g. "POTTER VALLEY PH" vs "POTTER VALLEY P H" is one such pair
in the file). Conclusion: redaction explains the PGE gap entirely.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
SCE: ~472 T3 substations not in loads / ~481 not in alt
─────────────────────────────────────────────
The same group drives both gaps (the overlap is almost complete).
73% of the T3-only substations have voltage_kv = NaN, versus essentially none
in the substations that have load profiles. The T3 entries without voltage all
carry a note_sub about deliverability (ICA interconnection capacity language),
suggesting they appear in SCE's ICA capacity table as "studied" nodes but do
not have metered load histories exposed via the scraper or bulk download.
58-63 of them are P.T. (pass-through) substations — these are switching nodes
that route load but have no individual meter.

Conclusion: T3 is padded with ICA deliverability entries for nodes that either
(a) have no metered load (P.T./switching), or (b) are transmission/capacity
study nodes that SCE tracks for interconnection purposes but does not publish
load profiles for.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
SCE: 30 alt (ICA_Layer) substations not in loads
─────────────────────────────────────────────
SUB_TYPE breakdown: 12 "A -- District Pole Top", 12 "S -- Sub-transmission",
4 "T -- Transmission", 2 "D -- Distribution".
Pole-top and sub/transmission substations typically serve bulk transmission or
very small distribution taps that are not included in the per-substation ICA
load-profile tables. They appear in the ICA_Layer geometry (so they have
lat/lon and capacity fields) but are absent from the Substation Load Profile
layer that the scraper and bulk download pull from.

Conclusion: type A/S/T nodes are geometry-registered but not load-profiled.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
SCE scrape vs bulk: 39 scrape-only / 3 bulk-only
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Bulk-only (3): "Globemaster P.T.", "Mailbox P.T.", "Masonry P.T." — all P.T.,
all year 2025-2026. The scrape only captured through 2024; the bulk download
was newer and picked up recently added P.T. substations. Temporal coverage
difference, not a content difference.

Scrape-only (39): spread across 2017-2024, no year concentration. Many are
P.T. substations (at least 10+ visible in the name list). These likely
represent substations that were present in the live API when scraped but were
not included in the bulk export (perhaps decommissioned, renamed, or excluded
from the bulk-download snapshot). No clear single cause — this is probably
messy data.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
Utility vs basin name-join gaps (B/C sections)
─────────────────────────────────────────────
The "only in source" substations that do not name-match the basin are
plausibly real substations not in the 2022 CEC dataset (basin is a 2022
snapshot; utility data covers 2017-2026). The spatial join shows that
name-matched pairs are consistently within 1 km (91% agreement at 0-1 km
for PGE/SCE), so the coordinate quality of matched pairs is high. The
unmatched fraction is not due to coordinate error — those substations are
genuinely absent from the basin dataset.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
Bottom line for source selection
─────────────────────────────────────────────
- Use alt (ICA_Layer) when you need lat/lon: its coordinate quality matches
  basin at median 0.1 km; it covers 95% of the substations that have load
  profiles.
- T3 adds ~480 substations over alt, but those extras are mostly P.T. nodes
  and ICA deliverability entries without load data — they are unlikely to be
  useful for load disaggregation.
- Bulk download is slightly smaller than scrape but covers 2025-2026; scrape
  has 39 extras spread across years with no clear pattern.
- PGE: the 42 attrs-only substations will never have load data (redacted);
  safe to treat attrs as a superset and loads as the operational subset.
